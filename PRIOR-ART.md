## Results, distinguishing published facts from deductions

- **Nikiforov (2017)**, *Merging the A- and Q-spectral theories*, *Applicable Analysis and Discrete Mathematics* **11**, 81–107; DOI `10.2298/AADM1701081N`, arXiv:`1607.03015`. The journal's **Problem 30**, p. 102, requests the minimum least `A_alpha` eigenvalue for `0 < alpha < 1/2`. **Proposition 38**, p. 105, gives the eigenvalues of `K_{r,s}`, so the candidate's radical formula is already printed. **Theorem 25** concerns the largest eigenvalue in the bipartite class, not the present optimization. Be careful that the 2016 arXiv and published 2017 numbering can differ (journal Proposition 38 versus preprint Proposition 37).
- **Even n: consequence of published ingredients.** The adjacency least-eigenvalue bound `lambda_min(A(G)) >= -sqrt(floor(n^2/4))` and equality only for the balanced complete bipartite graph are recalled in **Liu--Das--Shu (2020), Lemma 2.5**, *Discrete Mathematics* **343**, 111917, DOI `10.1016/j.disc.2020.111917`. Constantine (1985), *Lower bounds on the spectra of symmetric matrices with nonnegative entries*, *LAA* **65**, 171–178, DOI `10.1016/0024-3795(85)90095-3`, is antecedent in the adjacency direction. Combined with `A_alpha=(1-2alpha)A+alpha Q`, `Q` positive semidefinite, and Nikiforov's Proposition 38, this proves the even-order sharp bound and uniqueness. This is **not claimed new**. For odd n, `K_{p,q}` does not make the simultaneous adjacency-plus-Q inequality tight, so a different proof is needed.
- **Odd n and global uniqueness:** `certificates/problem30odd/REPORT.md` contains a complete sign-pattern deletion and strict Perron comparison proof for every real `0<alpha<1/2` and all `n>=2`. The exact K spectrum is published; the overall odd-order extremum/unique minimizer was **not found as an explicit published statement in this bounded search**. That phrase must not be strengthened to a priority or originality claim.
- **Lin--Xue--Shu (2018)**, *On the A_alpha-spectra of graphs*, *LAA* **556**, 210–219, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2018.07.003`, arXiv:`1709.00182`: **Theorem 3.3** treats an inequality in the *opposite* regime `alpha>1/2` for bipartite graphs; **Theorem 1.1** gives a separate higher-eigenvalue equality characterization. Neither establishes Problem 30 in the stated range.
- **Wang--Wong--Tian (2020)**, *Bounds for the largest and the smallest A_alpha eigenvalues of a graph in terms of vertex degrees*, *LAA* **590**, 210–223, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2019.12.039`: **Theorem 1.3** is a degree-sensitive lower bound for the least eigenvalue of bipartite graphs, with regular/semiregular equality cases. It is pertinent prior art but is not the displayed global odd-order statement.
- **Liu--Das--Sun--Shu (2020)**, *On the least eigenvalue of A_alpha-matrix of graphs*, *LAA* **586**, 347–376, DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2019.10.025`: its accessible abstract addresses least-eigenvalue bounds chiefly when `1/2 <= alpha <= 1`; full theorem-by-theorem text was **not available in the original audit**, so no unconditional non-overlap claim is warranted.
- **Bell--Cvetković--Rowlinson--Simić (2008), parts I and II**, *Graphs for which the least eigenvalue is minimal*, *LAA* **429**, 234–241 and 2168–2179, DOIs `10.1016/j.laa.2008.02.032` and `10.1016/j.laa.2008.06.018`: adjacency (`alpha=0`) problem for graphs of *fixed connected order and size*, with sign-vector and bipartite structure. Errata exist for part II (Stirling archive). These are precedents for, not solutions of, the unrestrained `A_alpha` runner-up or fixed-`(n,m)` question.
- **Stability and runner-up:** `certificates/p30stability/REPORT.md` supplies the explicit nonnegative ground-state deficit identity and all-n stability proof. The report found no identical quantitative bound or continuous-parameter runner-up theorem in its bounded search. Runner-up n=2,3 is proved for all parameters; n=4..7 is **exact only at alpha = 1/10,1/4,2/5,49/100** using integer Bareiss/Sylvester separation over the NetworkX atlas. nauty `geng` plus NumPy independently scans all classes through n=9 at those parameters, but this is **floating point** and is not an exact certificate for n=8,9 or a continuous-alpha theorem. The all-n runner-up remains a conjecture.
