### Mathematical and logical boundaries

Write \(p=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), \(q=\lceil n/2\rceil\), \(\beta=1-\alpha\), \(c=1-2\alpha>0\), and
\[
F_n\(\alpha\)=\frac{\alpha n-\sqrt{\alpha^2n^2+4pq\(1-2\alpha\)}}{2}.
\]
Distinguish **(i)** the calculated spectrum of the *candidate graph*, **(ii)** optimality *among bipartite graphs*, **(iii)** optimality *among all graphs*, and **(iv)** uniqueness. An article proving only (i), or proving an \(\alpha>1/2\) inequality, is not an earlier publication of (iii)–(iv) for odd (n).

For Claim 2, the particular Athanor assertion, transcribed from [p30stability certificate](https://github.com/iwasborninbali/paper-aalpha-least-eigenvalue/blob/main/certificates/p30stability/REPORT.md), is
\[
d_{\mathrm{edit}}(G,K_{p,q})\le n\sqrt{\beta n\varepsilon/c}+L_*\varepsilon
\quad (0\le\varepsilon<-F_n, \lambda_{\min}(A_\alpha(G))\le F_n+\varepsilon),
\]
where (\mu_*=2c(n-1)/n), (C_*=1+\beta\sqrt{n-1}(n-1)/\mu_*), and
\[
L_*=n^2(n-1)^2\left[1/\alpha+(n-1)(1+2C_*)/\mu_*\right].
\]
For larger \(\varepsilon\), the certificate uses the unconditional edit bound (n(n-1)/2). I found no previously published graph-edit-distance conclusion with these or comparable explicit \(n,\alpha,\varepsilon\) hypotheses. General stability/ground-state identities elsewhere would not alone count as this theorem.

For Claim 3, *second distinct minimum* means excluding the numerical value already taken at (K_{p,q}), not “second-smallest eigenvalue of (A_\alpha(G)) for a single graph.” The certificate explicitly states: (n=3) proved for every (\alpha\in(0,1/2)); (n=3,4,5,6,7) checked at **four** rational parameters (1/10,1/4,2/5,49/100); the claimed even/odd pattern for **all** orders and all real \(\alpha\) is **unproved**. At (n=3), the proposed odd “balanced minus one edge” gives \(K_2\cup K_1\). At \(\alpha=0\), (K_3) has the **same** least eigenvalue (-1) as \(K_2\cup K_1\), so a unique-graph statement cannot be carried unchanged across the boundary \(\alpha=0\). The 2012 adjacency article's abstract announces a second-minimum classification; it must be consulted before stating an adjacency priority claim.
